Arne Zetterström investigated hydrogen as a breathing gas for divers during wartime shortages of imported helium. His 1945 paper describes his proposal of September 1943 and an intermediate flushing mixture to precede the switch to hydrogen. An editorial introduction to his posthumous paper, published in Teknisk Tidskrift in December 1946, reports self-tests from the navy ship Belos at 40, 113, and 160 metres. It dates the fatal dive to 7 August 1945. According to that contemporary account, Zetterström gave satisfactory telephone reports during ascent before a line intended to prevent platform rotation was mistakenly winched in. The account attributes his death to decompression sickness following the rapid ascent.
