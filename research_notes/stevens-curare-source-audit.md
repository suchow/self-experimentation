# Stevens source audit

Checked 24 November 2026 for issue #3. The experiment entry remains unchanged.

## Original paper

Stevens et al. (1976), *Vision Research*, 16, 93–98. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/0042-6989(76)90082-1

Accessible OCR of the scanned paper: https://www.scribd.com/document/1054941104/05-Stevens-1976-Paralysis-of-the-Awake-Human-Visual-Perceptions

Methods, pp. 93–94, identifies the partial-paralysis observers as RCE, ACR, and JKS: Emerson, Rosenquist, and Stevens. JKS was the observer throughout the total-paralysis experiments. The final three used succinylcholine after a curare predose; two earlier curare experiments did not completely suppress voluntary eye movements.

A tourniquet protected his right arm from the drug. He communicated by hand flexion under a rehearsed protocol, including stopping the experiment. The entry’s statement that he could not signal unaided omits this crucial provision. Page 94 describes assisted ventilation and medical monitoring.

The abstract distinguishes displacement following successful eye movement under partial paralysis from displacement following intended movement under complete paralysis. The entry conflates these conditions. Page 95 also records no displacement in the first experiment; a uniform account obscures variation between sessions.

These findings come from OCR, which contains obvious transcription errors. Numerical doses and apparatus details require checking against page images before inclusion. The later studies are assessed below.

## Later studies

The PubMed record confirms the corrected Matin et al. citation: *Science*, 216(4542), 198–201, 9 April 1982, DOI 10.1126/science.7063881. Its six authors match the corrected reference already in the entry. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7063881/

The 1982 abstract, retrieved in a subsequent check, supports errors exceeding 20 degrees in darkness and accurate visual localization and pointing in an illuminated, structured field. It also reports persistent errors in auditory-to-visual matching under illumination. The entry should specify the visual tasks when describing restored accuracy. The full text and participant identities remain unverified. The identity of the unrelated chemistry reference has not been established; remove it only after documenting its provenance.

Whitham et al. (2011), abstract and Experimental Procedure, confirms six awake volunteers under full neuromuscular paralysis. Its abstract reports different experiences across participants and lighting conditions: environmental movement in one participant under illumination, and target-light movement in four under darkness. This supports the entry’s limited statement about variability. It does not establish a direct replication of the 1976 conditions. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3232712/

A second read-only review confirmed the 1976 OCR findings but did not obtain page images. Methods and Results describe overlapping experiment series differently; the neutral wording “an initial experiment” avoids assigning an unsupported overall session number. Numerical details remain pending image inspection.
