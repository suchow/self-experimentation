# Zetterström and Traver source audit

Checked 25 November 2026 for issue #3. Neither experiment entry has been changed.

## Zetterström

The editorial introduction to Zetterström’s posthumous paper in *Teknisk Tidskrift*, 21 December 1946, p. 1338, reports self-tests from *Belos* at 40, 113, and 160 metres. It dates the fatal dive to 7 August 1945. It describes satisfactory telephone reports during ascent before a line intended to prevent platform rotation was mistakenly winched in. It attributes death to decompression sickness following the rapid ascent. This account does not support the draft’s uninterrupted haul from the bottom or its categorical exclusion of hydrogen from the accident’s consequences.

Source: https://runeberg.org/tektid/1946/1350.html

Zetterström’s own 1945 paper, p. 176, describes unavailable helium imports, his September 1943 proposal, and an intermediate flushing mixture before switching to hydrogen. It does not establish a uniform 96/4 mixture throughout the trials.

Source: https://runeberg.org/tektid/1945/0188.html

I read both OCR pages independently. Runeberg marks them unproofread; page images remain unchecked. A separate reviewer found a 2007 archival history with additional sequence and mixture details, but those details await my review. Six dives, the first-dive depth, the precise gas composition, and hypoxia as a cause of death remain unresolved. A revision should attribute the contemporary accident account and omit the categorical closing verdict.

## Traver

Hinkle (2000), p. 18, explicitly interprets Traver’s published account as a classic case of delusory parasitosis. Shelomi (2013), abstract, likewise classifies the account and discusses possible retraction. These support named retrospective attribution. They do not establish a documented contemporaneous psychiatric diagnosis or justify an unattributed claim of general consensus.

Sources:
- https://portal.ct.gov/-/media/CAES/DOCUMENTS/Bed_Bugs/CIDS/DelusoryParasitosisNHinklepdf.pdf
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22173734/

I independently inspected rendered images of Traver’s original pp. 1–8. Page 1 confirms the university affiliation, two adult female relatives, Edward W. Baker’s identification of the submitted mites, symptoms beginning in 1934, and worsening in 1943. Pages 1–3 support the reported nighttime sensations and involvement of eyes, ears, and nostrils. Pages 5–6 support her account of the dermatologist, hospital stay, neurologist referral, and rejection of the need for his services. Pages 6–8 describe self-treatment, temporary relief without complete eradication, and reported migration after mercuric-iodide soap. These establish her reports, not the biological cause of the symptoms. No deliberate self-infestation appears in these pages.

Primary source: https://archive.org/download/biostor-76393/biostor-76393.pdf

The mayfly-authority description and nonburrowing-mite assertion remain unchecked. Preserve attribution to Traver for reported symptoms and treatment effects. Replace the broad consensus statement with named later interpretations.

### Biographical check

The Smithsonian Institution Archives finding aid for Record Unit 7298, Charles P. Alexander Papers, Series 8, printed p. 69 (PDF p. 71), describes Traver as an authority on New World mayflies and records faculty posts at Massachusetts from 1938 to 1962. This supports “an authority on New World mayflies”; the added ranking “leading” is unnecessary. The original report’s byline independently supports her zoology affiliation.

Source: https://sirismm.si.edu/EADpdfs/SIA.FARU7298.pdf

The nonburrowing-mite assertion remains unverified. Search results expose older taxonomic and clinical literature with differing interpretations; no biological verdict is drawn from those snippets.

### Fain specimen check

I inspected page images of Fain (1967), *Acarologia* 9, including printed pp. 181, 192, and 200–201. Page 192 describes a preparation supplied by Baker containing two specimens collected by Traver. Fain identifies the female as *D. pteronyssinus* and the male as *D. farinae*. Page 200 repeats the distinction between the specimens collected in September 1945 and September 1946. This supports a precise statement about reassessment of those specimens, not a claim that every submitted specimen belonged to one species.

The inspected passages do not establish the draft’s categorical nonburrowing assertion. They also do not establish a retrospective psychiatric diagnosis. Replace the unsupported biological clause with the documented specimen reassessment; retain Hinkle and Shelomi as the named sources of the later interpretation.

Source: https://www1.montpellier.inrae.fr/CBGP/acarologia/article.php?id=3629

PDF: https://www1.montpellier.inrae.fr/CBGP/acarologia/export_pdf.php?id=3629&typefile=1
